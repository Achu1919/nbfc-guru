## 📚 Daily Teaching — Saturday IBC: Section 7 after the 2026 amendment, and the creditor-led route that isn't switched on yet

### What is it?
When a corporate borrower defaults on at least ₹1 crore of financial debt, MFL can take it to the NCLT under **Section 7** of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code as a **financial creditor**. That's the normal IBC route for any lender.

The **IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026** (Act 6 of 2026, assent 6 April 2026) changed two things NBFC lenders care about. It tightened the Section 7 route, and it created a new **creditor-initiated insolvency resolution process (CIIRP)** that lets lenders start insolvency *without* going to the tribunal first. ([Amendment Act](https://nclt.gov.in/sites/default/files/Act%26rules/The%20Insolvency%20and%20Bankruptcy%20Code%20%28Amendment%29%20Act%2C%202026.pdf))

The catch, and the point of today's lesson: **the Section 7 changes are in force; the CIIRP chapter, on the primary text, is not.**

This connects to the news. IBBI's two circulars yesterday are about *how* claims get filed and how the RP runs a real-estate CIRP. Today's lesson is about how MFL gets a borrower into that process in the first place.

Two Saturdays ago (17 Oct) we covered the **FSP track**: how an NBFC like MFL could itself be taken into insolvency, which only RBI can start under Section 227. Today is the other side: MFL as the creditor.

### The Technical Details

**1. Section 7 as it now reads (in force from 26 May 2026)**

- **Who and how much.**
  - Any financial creditor can file, alone or jointly.
  - The minimum default is **₹1 crore**, set under Section 4 by notification S.O. 1205(E) of 24 March 2020.
- **The 14-day clock** (Section 7(5) as substituted by s.4 of the 2026 Act).
  - The NCLT "shall, within fourteen days" admit the application if a default has occurred, the application is complete and the proposed RP has no disciplinary case pending.
  - Otherwise it rejects. If it misses 14 days, it must record its reasons in writing.
- **Default is enough.** Explanation I: once those conditions are met, "no other ground shall be considered" to reject. This writes into the statute the line that the tribunal shouldn't weigh the borrower's other arguments at the admission stage.
- **Information utility record.** Explanation II: if a **financial institution** files a record of default from an information utility (in practice, NeSL) with its application, that record is "sufficient" for the NCLT to find default. For a lender, the IU entry now does the evidentiary work.
- **Withdrawal is harder** (new s.12A).
  - A case can be withdrawn only with **90%** of CoC voting share.
  - It can't be withdrawn **before the CoC is formed** or **after the first invitation for resolution plans**.
  - The NCLT decides within 30 days.
  - So settling with a borrower after filing now has a narrow window.

**2. CIIRP: the new route on paper (Chapter IV-A, ss.58A–58K)**

- **Who can start it.** A financial creditor "belonging to such class of financial institutions as may be notified by the Central Government" (s.58B(1)).
- **Against whom.** Only corporate debtors the Government notifies by assets, income, class of creditors or debt (s.58A(1)).
- **Excluded debtors.** Anyone already in IBC proceedings, or who has been through CIIRP, pre-pack or a completed CIRP in the past three years (s.58A(2)).
- **Steps** (s.58B(2)–(4)):
  - Get approval from notified-class creditors holding at least **51%** of that class's debt.
  - Give the borrower at least **30 days** to make a representation.
  - Take a second 51% approval within 30 days of that representation.
  - Then appoint an RP **directly**, with no tribunal order needed.
  - The process starts on the RP's public announcement.
- **Borrower's remedy.** The borrower can object to the NCLT within **30 days** of commencement (s.58C).
- **Management.** Unlike CIRP, the board stays in charge, but the RP attends board meetings and can reject board resolutions (s.58F).
- **Clock.** **150 days**, extendable once by up to **45** (s.58D). If no plan is approved, the case converts to CIRP.

**3. Why CIIRP isn't usable yet**

- MCA notification **S.O. 2625(E)** of 22 May 2026 brought the Act into force from **26 May 2026** for a listed set of sections.
- It lists "sections 35 to 39" and "section 41". It **skips section 40**, which is the section that inserts Chapter IV-A. ([Gazette](https://nclt.gov.in/sites/default/files/Act%26rules/The%20Insolvency%20and%20Bankruptcy%20Code%20%28Amendment%29%20Act%2C%202026%20Enforment%20Date%20Notification.pdf))
- Section 42 (group insolvency) is skipped too.
- LiveLaw reported on 26 May that CIIRP had been "operationalised" ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelawbiz.com/compliance/ibc/centre-notifies-key-ibc-amendment-provisions-operationalises-creditor-initiated-insolvency-resolution-process-535725)). **The gazette text doesn't support that.**
- Even after section 40 is notified, nothing happens until the Government notifies the **eligible lender class** (s.58B(1)) and the **eligible borrowers** (s.58A(1)), and IBBI finalises its CIIRP regulations. So far those exist only as a discussion paper ([IBBI discussion paper](https://ibbi.gov.in/uploads/whatsnew/fb032d2da0f5228ad2f28afbc7f18183.pdf)).
- I found no later notification. Treat CIIRP as **not available** until one appears.

### Why It Matters
- **For MFL's non-gold books** (MSME, LAP, corporate loans), the faster Section 7 is the stronger stick. A 14-day admission clock, IU records as proof, and a hard-to-withdraw case give a borrower more reason to settle *before* filing.
- **For the gold book**, IBC barely matters. Gold-loan borrowers are mostly individuals, and IBC's individual-insolvency part isn't in force for them. The pledged gold is enforced by auction, not through the NCLT.
- **CIIRP's risk is the 51% vote.** If NBFCs make the notified class, MFL could be outvoted by bigger lenders into, or out of, a creditor-led process on a shared borrower. If NBFCs *don't* make the class, banks get a faster tool than MFL has.

### Muthoot Fincorp Context
- **MFL as creditor.** Only borrowers with ≥₹1 crore in default qualify, so this is the MSME/LAP/corporate tail, not gold. The practical step is to make sure every eligible loan's default is **recorded with NeSL** promptly. Under Explanation II, that record is what makes a Section 7 filing quick.
- **MFL as debtor.** As an NBFC with assets far above ₹500 cr, MFL can be put into insolvency only by **RBI** under the FSP route, not by a creditor under Section 7 (covered 17 Oct).
- **The FP&A angle:**
  - The ECL model's recovery assumptions for IBC-bound accounts can reasonably assume faster admission now.
  - Don't build CIIRP into recovery timelines until it is notified.
  - A recovery planned on the IBC route may also have a SARFAESI option; compare the two before assuming a timeline.
- **One thing to check:** whether the legal and collections team has a list of ≥₹1 crore defaulted non-gold accounts with NeSL records attached.

### Quiz yourself
1. A company's only debt is ₹80 lakh owed to MFL, all in default. Can MFL file under Section 7? *(**No.** The minimum default is **₹1 crore** (S.O. 1205(E)).)*
2. After admission, the borrower offers MFL a full settlement before the CoC is formed. Can the case be withdrawn? *(**No.** New s.12A bars withdrawal **before the CoC is constituted**; after that it needs **90%** of CoC voting share, and only until the first invitation for plans.)*
3. Can MFL start a CIIRP against an MSME borrower on Monday? *(**No.** Section 40 of the 2026 Act, which inserts Chapter IV-A, wasn't in S.O. 2625(E). Even once it is, the eligible lender class and borrowers have to be notified first.)*
4. Which single document does the 2026 amendment make "sufficient" proof of default for a financial institution's Section 7 application? *(A **record of default from an information utility**, Explanation II to s.7(5).)*
5. Who can start insolvency against MFL itself? *(Only **RBI**, as the appropriate regulator under the FSP Rules made under Section 227, since MFL's assets exceed ₹500 cr.)*
