Morning, Achu. It's Friday, the thirtieth of October.

The Economic Times story, picked up by Moneycontrol yesterday, talks about roughly a hundred and fifty complaints. Most of the victims are lenders with turnover of about a thousand crore or less. One Delhi company apparently lost seven and a half crore, and then got hit again for another seven crore and change a few days later. The trick is simple, and that's the uncomfortable part. Attackers steal the keys a lender uses to talk to its payment gateway or partner bank, and then they call the payout API from machines the system already trusts. KYC never runs. Neither does the sanction check.

CERT-In flagged this exact pattern on the ninth of October. That ties in with today's teaching, which is RBI's new cyber and technology directions from July. We're Middle Layer, so we get the strictest chapter. That means a CISO who doesn't report to IT and doesn't carry business targets, a vulnerability scan every six months, a pen test every year, and the one I'd remember: if something goes wrong, RBI hears about it on DAKSH within six hours. For a gold lender, money moving fast is what customers come to us for. It's also the bit that's exposed. It'd be worth asking whether anyone in finance looks at payouts by hour and channel each day. That's the cheapest early warning there is.

Our NCD is doing fine. Tranche One reached one point six seven times by five yesterday evening, up from one point four five the day before. HNIs are past two times and retail is at one point four two.
