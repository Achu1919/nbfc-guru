## 📚 Daily Teaching — Friday digital: the 2026 Cyber/Tech Directions, read through today's ₹300 cr API-fraud story

### What is it?
RBI has replaced its old NBFC IT Framework (2017) and IT Governance Master Direction (2023) with one rulebook: the **Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Cybersecurity, Technology: Risk, Resilience and Assurance Framework) Directions, 2026**, `RBI/DoS/2026-27/461`, `DoS.CO.CSITEG.55/31.01.015/2026-27`, dated **31 July 2026**, in force **immediately** (para 2). ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13592&Mode=0))

The rules are tiered by layer. Small Base Layer NBFCs below ₹500 cr get a light baseline (Chapter III), larger Base Layer NBFCs get Chapter IV, and **Middle, Upper and Top Layer NBFCs get the full Chapter V** (para 3(4)). MFL is Middle Layer, so Chapter V applies to it.

Today's news is why this is worth an hour: an estimated ₹300 cr was stolen from mostly small NBFCs in two months through hijacked payment and disbursement APIs. Most of those victims sit in Chapter III or IV. MFL sits in Chapter V, which is the strictest tier.

### The Technical Details
All references are to the 2026 Directions ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13592&Mode=0)), Chapter V unless noted.

**Who owns it**
- **Board (paras 68–69).** The board approves IT, information-security, cybersecurity, BCP and cyber-crisis policies, and reviews them at least **annually**.
- **IT Strategy Committee (paras 70–73).** At least **three directors**, chaired by an independent director with **seven years'** IT experience. It meets at least **quarterly**. It must make sure IT-security budgets match the NBFC's "digital depth" and threat environment (73(5)), and it approves the need-based access standards (73(8)).
- **IT Steering Committee (para 75).** Senior management from IT and business. Meets **quarterly**.
- **Information Security Committee (para 77).** Run by the CISO. Reviews cyber incidents and IS audit findings.
- **CISO (paras 81–82).** Preferably **GM rank**. The CISO has **no reporting line to the Head of IT and no business targets**, and reports to the ED or equivalent who oversees risk. The CISO briefs the board, RMCB or ITSC on cyber risk at least **quarterly** (82(7)).

**What would have stopped an API heist**
- **Access (paras 113–115).** Access only where there is a valid business need. Privileged users are logged and reviewed. **MFA for privileged users** of critical systems and for critical activities, based on risk assessment.
- **Audit trails (paras 110–111).** Logs good enough to serve as forensic evidence, and **regular monitoring** of trails and logs to catch unauthorised activity. This is the control that spots an unusual burst of payouts.
- **Third parties (para 117).** Vendor risk assessment proportionate to materiality. Payment aggregators, gateways and partner-bank connectors are third parties.
- **No manual touch (para 119).** No manual changes to data moving between critical applications, so straight-through processing.
- **Testing (paras 121, 129).** Vulnerability assessment at least **every six months**. Penetration testing at least **every 12 months** for critical systems and customer-facing DMZ systems. DR drills at least **half-yearly** for critical systems, running a full working day on the DR site (para 131).

**When it goes wrong**
- **Report within six hours (para 141).** A cyber incident is reported to RBI on **DAKSH within six hours of detection**, and CERT-In is notified proactively. HFCs report to NHB instead.
- **Response plan (paras 139–143).** A written incident-response and recovery policy, forensic root-cause analysis, and a communication plan covering the board, senior management and customers.

**Who checks it**
- **IS audit (paras 149–154).** The Audit Committee oversees a separate IS audit function and approves the IS Audit Policy, reviewed **annually**. Audit is risk-based, with continuous auditing encouraged for critical systems.

### Why It Matters
In the ET-reported pattern, attackers didn't break the loan system. They stole credentials and then called the disbursement API from infrastructure the lender already trusted, so KYC, sanction and limit checks never ran. That's a failure of access control, monitoring and vendor control, which is exactly what paras 110–119 cover.

For a gold lender the payout is the product: cash or a bank credit within minutes of appraisal. Speed is the selling point and also the attack surface. The six-hour DAKSH clock means a breach becomes a regulatory event before the day is out, not just an IT event.

### Muthoot Fincorp Context
- **Scope.** MFL is Middle Layer, so all of Chapter V applies: board-level ITSC, independent CISO, quarterly cyber briefings, half-yearly VA and DR drills, and the six-hour DAKSH clock. Muthoot Microfin is a separate NBFC and is assessed on its own layer.
- **The FP&A angle:**
  - **IT-security spend is a regulated budget line.** Para 73(5) asks the ITSC to confirm the cybersecurity budget matches the NBFC's digital depth and threat environment. When the FY28 plan trims opex, that line needs an ITSC-defensible rationale.
  - **Fraud losses and recoveries.** A payout-API loss is an operational-risk loss. It hits provisions and, if material, LODR disclosure for a listed-debt issuer like MFL. It's worth knowing whether MFL's cyber insurance covers funds-transfer fraud through third-party APIs.
  - **Velocity metrics.** Para 147 asks for metrics. A daily view of disbursement count and value by channel and hour, against a normal band, is the simple control that would have caught the small-NBFC pattern early.
- **One thing to check this week:** that the CISO's next quarterly note to the board or RMCB (82(7)) explicitly addresses CERT-In CIAD-2026-0046, including credential rotation for gateway and partner-bank keys.

### Quiz yourself
1. MFL detects unauthorised payouts at 10:00 IST. What's the RBI deadline, and where? *(**16:00 IST the same day**, on **DAKSH**, within six hours of detection. CERT-In is notified too (para 141).)*
2. Can the CISO report to the CTO if the CTO is very senior? *(**No.** The CISO can't have a direct reporting line to the Head of IT and can't carry business targets (para 81).)*
3. How often must MFL pen-test its customer-facing loan app? *(At least **once every 12 months**, with vulnerability assessment every six months (para 121).)*
4. A Base Layer NBFC with ₹400 cr of assets: which chapter? *(**Chapter III**, the baseline requirements (para 3(2)).)*
5. Who approves the IS Audit Policy? *(The **Audit Committee of the Board**, reviewed at least annually (para 150).)*
