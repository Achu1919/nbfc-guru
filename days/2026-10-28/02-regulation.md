### What is it?
When a Central or State Government **declares** a calamity, the RBI lets an NBFC restructure the loans of affected borrowers **without the account turning NPA**, but only inside a strict window and at a provisioning cost. Before 1 Jul 2026, there was no single NBFC rulebook for this. Chapter VI-A is now that rulebook.

### The Technical Details

**A. What counts (para 3(8A), 122A)**
- A "natural calamity" is an event recognised under the **NDRF/SDRF** framework. Riots and disturbances that cause economic loss are covered *mutatis mutandis*.
- The trigger is a **declaration by the Central or State Government**. A bad monsoon on its own doesn't trigger anything.
- The relief decision is taken in a **special SLBC/UTLBC/DCC meeting**, and its convenor communicates it to NBFCs (122C). The NBFC must then **publicise** it through brochures, banners, newspaper ads and field staff (122D).

**B. Who is eligible (122F–122H)**
- The account must be **Standard** and **not more than 30 days in default on any facility** with the NBFC, measured on the **date of occurrence**. If that date can't be pinned down, the date of declaration is used instead.
- Ineligible borrowers can still be resolved under the rest of the Directions (122G).
- The chapter does **not** apply to an NBFC's refinance portfolio (122H).

**C. The clock (122I–122J)**
- **Invoke within 45 days** of the declaration, and **implement within 135 days**.
- The NBFC can act **suo motu** on the SLBC/DCC recommendation, without waiting for a borrower request. Invocation is then deemed. The borrower must be told and can **opt out at any time up to day 135**.

**D. What the plan can contain (122K–122M)**
- It can reschedule payments, convert accrued or future interest into a separate facility, or add **fresh finance**. Each of these needs a viability assessment.
- Expected insurance proceeds can be counted in the plan, and fresh loans can be sanctioned **before** the claim is paid.
- Government interest subvention and prompt-repayment incentives must be passed on "without any exception" (122N).

**E. The accounting (IRAC paras 27A–27B, 36D–36F, 40A–40B)**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Classification on implementation | Standard **stays Standard**. An account that **slipped to NPA between the calamity and implementation is upgraded to Standard** (27A) |
| Restructured again for a later calamity | Stays **Standard** (27B) |
| Extra provision | **5% of outstanding debt**, on top of normal provisions and capped at 100% (36D) |
| Each repeat restructuring | **Another 5%** per instance (36E) |
| Write-back | Once the borrower repays **≥20%** of outstanding debt without slipping to NPA and without another restructuring (36F) |
| Interest income | **Accrual** for a first plan (40A); **cash basis** on repeat restructurings (40B) |

**F. Reporting (122Q–122R)**
- Relief data goes on the **CIMS portal half-yearly**, within **30 days** of 30 Sep and 31 Mar.
- If no relief was extended, a **NIL** statement is still required.

**G. Illustration (hypothetical numbers, not MFL data)**

Suppose a State declares drought in a district on day 0. An NBFC has **₹10 cr** of eligible Standard MSME and personal loans there and restructures all of it by day 120.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Normal standard-asset provision | Unchanged |
| Additional specific provision, 5% (36D) | **₹50 lakh**, charged in the quarter of implementation |
| If borrowers repay ₹2 cr (20%) cleanly | ₹50 lakh written back (36F) |
| If a second calamity forces another restructuring | A further 5% on what is then outstanding (36E), with interest on a **cash** basis (40B) |

The trade-off: you pay 5% up front to keep a borrower Standard who might otherwise have rolled into the 90+ DPD NPA bucket and attracted a 10% sub-standard provision (IRAC 32(1)). That only makes sense if the borrower is genuinely viable.

### Why It Matters
- **The large lenders are choosing the other route.** By cutting fresh credit, Shriram, M&M and Bajaj are protecting new business. Chapter VI-A is about the *existing* book: it lets an NBFC avoid an NPA cliff for borrowers who were current when the rain failed.
- **Gold loans rarely need this.** A gold borrower with stress usually renews, part-pays or lets the pledge go to auction, and the collateral can be sold. The 13 Oct note said as much ("Gold book almost never uses this"). The chapter matters most for **MFI, MSME and unsecured** books, which is where Muthoot Microfin sits.
- **It is a timing tool, so FP&A owns part of it.** The 5% hits P&L in the quarter of implementation, while the write-back comes quarters later. A drought declared in November could put a Q3 provision line into MFL's consolidated numbers via the subsidiary.

### Key Numbers

| Item | Number |
|------|--------|
| Eligibility | Standard, and **≤30 days** in default on any facility at the date of occurrence |
| Invocation / implementation | **45 / 135 days** from declaration |
| Borrower opt-out (suo motu cases) | Any time up to **day 135** |
| Additional specific provision | **5%** per restructuring, capped at 100% |
| Write-back trigger | **20%** of debt repaid, with no NPA and no re-restructuring |
| CIMS return | Half-yearly, within **30 days** of 30 Sep / 31 Mar; NIL if none |
| Effective from | **1 Jul 2026** |

### Recent Changes
- Chapter VI-A and the matching IRAC paragraphs were **inserted on 29 Apr 2026 and took effect on 1 Jul 2026**. Para 122B: accounts that had already received relief before that date stay under the old norms, but any *fresh* resolution follows Chapter VI-A.
- The RBC amendment that took effect on the same date gives NBFCs discretion to waive certain fees for calamity-hit borrowers (para 105A; noted on 22 Oct).

### Quiz yourself
1. Karnataka's monsoon was 32% below normal. Can MFL restructure under Chapter VI-A today? *(**Only after** a Central/State declaration and the SLBC/DCC decision. A rainfall deficit alone is not a trigger, under 122A and 122E.)*
2. A borrower was 45 DPD on the date of occurrence. Eligible? *(**No.** The limit is ≤30 days in default on any facility (122F). Use the other resolution provisions instead (122G).)*
3. An account slipped to NPA between the calamity and the plan's implementation on day 100. What is its classification after implementation? *(**Upgraded to Standard** (27A). After that, normal IRAC rules apply.)*
4. ₹4 cr restructured once, then again after a second calamity. Total additional specific provision? *(**5% + 5%** of outstanding at each instance (36D–36E), and income is on a cash basis after the repeat (40B).)*
5. MFL gave no calamity relief in H1 FY27. Is anything due? *(**Yes.** A **NIL** statement on CIMS within 30 days of 30 Sep (122Q–122R).)*
