## 📚 Daily Teaching — Tuesday IRAC / NPA: the day-end clock (when exactly does a loan become SMA or NPA, and when does it come back?)

Day **71** of the curriculum (Day 1 = 18 Aug 2026), still in refresher mode. Tuesday is the **IRAC / NPA** slot. Recent Tuesday-slot angles:
- the ECL prudential floor;
- the IRAC refresher with the 1 Oct SNFA overlay (20 Oct), which listed the borrower-level upgradation rule in one line;
- bullet vs EMI "overdue" (25 Oct recap).

None of them dealt with the **clock itself**: which calendar date an account becomes overdue, SMA-1, SMA-2 or NPA, and exactly what brings it back. That clock decides which loans fall on which side of the **30 Sep** Q2 cut-off, so it matters for the 14 Nov pack.

Source for everything below: **Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning) Directions, 2025**, `RBI/DOR/2025-26/356`, `DOR.STR.REC.No.275/21.04.048/2025-26`, dated 28 Nov 2025, updated as on 1 Oct 2026. ([RBI MD](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12948))

### What is it?
RBI doesn't let an NBFC choose when to "look" at a loan. Classification runs as part of the **day-end process** for each calendar date, and the date stamped on the account is **that calendar date, whatever time the batch actually runs** (paras 18–19). Once an account is NPA, it comes back to standard only when **every rupee of arrears on every facility of that borrower** is paid (paras 24–25).

### The Technical Details

**A. Overdue (para 11(2), para 18)**
- Any amount is "overdue" if it isn't paid on the **due date fixed by the NBFC**.
- The account is flagged overdue in the **day-end process for the due date**, "irrespective of the time of running such processes". A batch that runs at 2 AM on the next day still stamps the due date.

**B. SMA and NPA dating (para 19 and RBI's Illustration I)**
- SMA and NPA classification also happen in the day-end process, and the classification date is **the calendar date for which day-end is run**.
- RBI's own worked example: the due date is 31 Mar 2021 and full dues are not received before day-end. Then:
  - the account is **overdue** on 31 Mar;
  - it is **SMA-1** on 30 Apr, after 30 days of being continuously overdue;
  - it is **SMA-2** on 30 May;
  - it is **NPA** on 29 Jun.
- The phrase that matters is **"continuously overdue"**. The clock counts from the oldest unpaid due date and runs until the arrears are cleared.

**C. What NPA means for MFL (para 51, NBFC-ML)**
- MFL is a Middle Layer NBFC, so para 51 applies. An asset is an NPA if:
  - interest has stayed overdue for **more than 90 days**; or
  - a term-loan instalment or its interest has been overdue for more than 90 days; or
  - a demand or call loan has been overdue for more than 90 days from demand.
- Para 51(8): once any facility is NPA, **the whole balance to that borrower, including accrued interest**, is NPA. Para 23 says the same thing: classification is **borrower-wise, not facility-wise**.
- After that: **sub-standard** for up to **12 months** as an NPA (para 52), then **doubtful** (para 53). Sub-standard carries a **10%** general provision (para 32(1)).

**D. Coming back (paras 22, 24, 25)**
- NPA → standard **only if the entire arrears of interest and principal are paid** (para 24).
- With more than one facility, **all arrears on all facilities** must be paid (para 25).
- **Rescheduling alone does not upgrade** (para 22). That needs the Resolution of Stressed Assets Directions.

**E. Income (para 38)**
- On NPA, income is recognised **only when actually realised**. Income booked earlier and still unrealised is **reversed**.

**F. Telling the borrower (paras 15–17)**
- For loans sanctioned on or after **31 Dec 2021**, the loan agreement must state:
  - the exact due dates and repayment frequency;
  - the principal/interest split;
  - **examples of SMA/NPA dates** (para 15).
- The website must explain overdue, SMA, NPA and upgradation **"with specific reference to day-end process"**, and front-line staff must explain them at sanction, disbursal and renewal (para 17).

**G. Illustration for the Q2 book (hypothetical loans, not MFL data)**

Apply RBI's own counting to the 30 Sep 2026 cut-off:

| Oldest unpaid due date | SMA-1 (day-end) | SMA-2 (day-end) | NPA (day-end) | In the 30 Sep 2026 book as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Jul 2026 | 1 Aug | 31 Aug | **30 Sep** | **NPA** |
| 3 Jul 2026 | 2 Aug | 1 Sep | 1 Oct | **SMA-2** (standard) |
| Paid in full on 30 Sep, before day-end | — | — | — | Standard, with no overdue flag |

One day apart in the due date moves a loan across the GNPA line for the whole quarter. A payment that lands **after** the 30 Sep day-end has run does not change the 30 Sep stamp.

Upgradation example: a borrower has two gold loans, both NPA. They pay off all the arrears on loan A. **Both stay NPA** until loan B's arrears are cleared as well (para 25).

### Why It Matters
- **GNPA is a dating exercise before it's a credit exercise.** The Q2 GNPA% and the SMA-1/SMA-2 split in the LODR notes and investor deck are only as good as the day-end stamp. If the batch dates by "run date" rather than "calendar date", a whole day's cohort shifts.
- **Upgrades are where numbers get flattered.** A partial payment that clears the oldest instalment can stop the clock on that dues line. It does **not** upgrade an NPA borrower unless all arrears on all facilities are cleared (paras 24–25). Any "cured" pool in the Q2 roll-forward should pass that test.
- **It's a conduct rule too.** Paras 15–17 put SMA/NPA dating into the loan agreement and the branch. If a customer disputes their classification, the examples given at sanction are the first thing an inspector or ombudsman will read.

### Key Numbers

| Item | Number |
|------|--------|
| Overdue | Not paid on the due date (para 11(2)) |
| SMA-1 / SMA-2 / NPA (RBI illustration) | Day **30 / 60 / 90** of being continuously overdue, at day-end |
| NBFC-ML NPA test (para 51) | Overdue **> 90 days** |
| Sub-standard (para 52) | NPA for **≤ 12 months** |
| Sub-standard provision (para 32(1)) | **10%** of total outstanding |
| Disclosure cohort (para 15) | Loans sanctioned on or after **31 Dec 2021** |
| Upgradation (paras 24–25) | **All** arrears on **all** facilities |

### Quiz yourself
1. A gold loan's interest fell due on 2 Jul 2026 and was still unpaid at day-end on 30 Sep. Is it in the Q2 GNPA? *(**Yes.** By RBI's own counting it turns NPA on the 30 Sep day-end run.)*
2. The day-end batch for 30 Sep finished at 1:30 AM on 1 Oct. Which date goes on accounts that slipped in that run? *(**30 Sep.** The date is the calendar date for which day-end is run, not the time it ran, under paras 18–19.)*
3. An NPA borrower clears all arrears on one of their two loans. Upgrade? *(**No.** All arrears on all facilities must be cleared, under para 25.)*
4. Interest of ₹40,000 was accrued before the loan went NPA and is still unpaid. What happens to it? *(It is **reversed**, and future income is recognised only when realised, under para 38.)*
5. Which loans must have SMA/NPA date examples in the agreement itself? *(Those sanctioned on or after **31 Dec 2021**. Older loans are covered at renewal or review, under paras 15–16.)*
