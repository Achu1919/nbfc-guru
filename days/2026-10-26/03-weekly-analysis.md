## 🏛️ Regulatory & Policy Watch

**1. MPC minutes (published 21 Oct): no cut in sight.** The 5–7 Oct meeting voted unanimously to raise repo 25 bps to 5.50% and moved the stance to "calibrated tightening". Two members, Nagesh Kumar and Ram Singh, backed the hike but opposed the stance change. FY27 CPI forecast is 5.2% and GDP 7.1% ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63839), [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/rbi-mpc-minutes-signal-rate-hike-or-pause-2-members-oppose-stance-change-14056907.html)).
- *Direct impact:* bank term loans and CP reprice upward with a lag. Bajaj Finance has already said H2 NIM compression may exceed its 10–15 bps guide ([CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/bajaj-finance-share-price-q2-result-reaction-fy27-guidance-aum-margin-repo-rate-buy-sell-target-outlook-20015660.htm)).
- *Second order:* the two dissents on stance mean the tightening bias is not unanimous. If oil cools, December could be a pause, not a hike.
- *MFL:* use repo-hike scenarios, not cut scenarios, for H2 cost of funds. Retail NCD coupons (8.56–9.25% on Tranche I) are set for the life of the paper, so the bank-line book is where the repricing shows up.

**2. Governor on Upper Layer listing (speech, 23 Oct).** Governor Malhotra said UL NBFCs, and CICs that use public funds, must register and list. He was backing RBI's treatment of Tata Sons ([ET](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/company/corporate-trends/rbi-governor-backs-tata-sons-classification-says-large-nbfcs-must-follow-listing-norms/articleshow/135149254.cms)).
- *MFL:* this is a speech, not a circular. MFL is Middle Layer and not on the UL list, so the listing mandate does not apply to it. Muthoot Finance (peer) is UL and already listed. MFL's own IPO plan (₹3,000 cr fresh-issue DRHP, filed August) is voluntary ([Fortune India](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/ipo/gold-lender-muthoot-fincorp-files-drhp-for-3000-crore-fresh-issue-ipo/153754)).

**3. RBI Bulletin study, "Performance of the NBFC Sector: Review for 2025" (23 Oct).** NBFCs' share of financial-sector assets fell to 12.7% (Mar 2025) from 13.9% (Mar 2020). UL+ML loans grew 24% to Dec 2025, gold-jewellery loans were up nearly 60%, GNPA was 2.7%, NNPA 0.9% and CRAR 25.2%. Borrowings rose 23.3%, more than 77% of them from banks and market instruments ([BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/nbfcs-lose-ground-despite-stronger-profitability-better-asset-quality-rbi-study/article71617268.ece), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/banking/nbfcs-reliance-on-bank-borrowings-rises-amid-strong-credit-growth-rbi-126102301611_1.html)).
- *Second order:* this is staff research, not a rule. But "bank interconnectedness" is the language RBI used before it raised bank risk weights on NBFC loans in 2023. I'd watch for it in the December FSR.

**4. Circulars actually issued this week.** RBI/2026-27/296 (19 Oct) updated three UNSC 1267 entries for UAPA screening. Every other RBI circular in the week was bank-only (capital adequacy, leverage ratio) or FEMA compounding. SEBI's only circular was on research-analyst call records ([RBI notifications](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx), [SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/home/HomeAction.do?doListing=yes&sid=1&ssid=7&smid=0)).
- *MFL:* KYC/FIU must load the 19 Oct screening file. There is no P&L impact.

MFL's lowest rating (CRISIL AA) would show as "very low" risk on the next tranche ([SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/oct-2026/introduction-of-credit-risk-o-meter-as-an-additional-disclosure-mechanism-for-debt-securities_105081.html)). ## ⚠️ Asset Quality Deep Dive

- **Gold: the numbers so far.** Bajaj: gold Stage 2 up ₹1,047 cr QoQ and gold GNPA 0.70% from 0.35% ([EconoTimes](https://econotimes.com/Bajaj-Finance-Ends-Short-Term-Gold-Loan-Schemes-as-Credit-Risk-Rises-1757807)). Shriram: gold GS3 3.12% (2.28% a year ago). IIFL: gold bad loans 0.9% from 0.6% in Q1; Fedbank Stage 3 0.5% from 0.1% ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/news/gold-loan-stress-nbfc-piramal-bajaj-finance-precious-metal-11792580129138.html)).
- **Two explanations, and both may be true.** Piramal reads it as borrower stress: 15–23% of disbursements went to borrowers 90+ DPD elsewhere, and 35% of gold customers now borrow from a second lender. Shriram reads it as product design: EMI and interest-servicing gold loans create monthly due dates for customers used to bullet repayment. Its LTV including accrued interest is about 72–73% ([NDTV Profit](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/markets/why-shriram-finance-wants-to-double-down-on-gold-loans-despite-repayment-red-flags-12228710)). The system-wide counterpoint is CIBIL's gold-loan delinquency of about 0.3% ([CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/business/finance/gff-gold-loans-second-largest-credit-segment-1-in-5-borrowers-npa-elsewhere-cibil-19987383.htm)).
- **Collateral cushion.** MCX gold is more than 20% below this year's ₹1.81 lakh peak ([BusinessLine live blog](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/sensex-nifty50-today-stock-market-highlights-23rd-october-2026/article71611889.ece)). On a one-year loan written near the peak at 75% LTV, a 20% fall in gold leaves almost no cushion once accrued interest is added. That is arithmetic, not a forecast.
- **Elsewhere asset quality is improving.** M&M Finance GS2+3 at 8.1% is a multi-year low. CreditAccess PAR 90+ is 1.1%. RBI's study puts sector GNPA at 2.7%.
- **ARC / stressed assets.** Sammaan Capital and Sammaan Finserve put stressed portfolios up for sale again. The data room is open 23 Oct–28 Nov and bids are due 30 Nov ([InsolvencyTracker](https://insolvencytracker.in/2026/10/22/sammaan-capital-subsidiary-put-stressed-loans-on-the-block-again-six-months-after-%E2%82%B95000-crore-arcil-sale/)). ## 💡 Analyst's Take

The headline numbers this week were very good: profit growth of 27% to 299% almost everywhere. The stocks still fell, and I think the market has the order right. Q2 profits come from a period of falling credit costs and cheap money that is now over. The 10-year has sold off for ten straight weeks, the MPC has ruled out a cut, and Brent is near $117. The real question for H2 is how much margin compression shows up, and the companies with floating-rate liabilities (M&M Finance at about 48%) were punished first.

Gold is the bigger shift. For two years gold loans were sold to investors as the safe secured product: low GNPA, LTV cushion, auction as a backstop. This week three diversified lenders reported rising gold delinquencies at the same moment gold had its worst week since June. That change in the story is what pulled IIFL, Manappuram and Muthoot Finance (peer) down 9–14%. I don't think it means gold books are about to blow up. Shriram's EMI explanation is plausible, and CIBIL's 0.3% system delinquency is still low. But the market will now price gold lenders on collateral cover and repeat-borrower mix, not on growth. That matters for any gold lender coming to the market, MFL included.

For MFL's leadership, three things this week. First, have the Q2 gold Stage 2/3 split by repayment type (bullet vs EMI or interest-servicing) ready before anyone asks, because analysts will apply Shriram's question to every gold book. Second, re-run LTV migration at ₹1.40 lakh and ₹1.35 lakh per 10g (scenarios, not forecasts) for loans booked near the peak; CARE puts gold at about 67% of MFL's consolidated AUM. Third, keep Tranche I tracking against the ₹150 cr minimum, not the ₹500 cr headline. Jio refunds on 27 Oct should release retail cash, but Jio's listing on 28 Oct will compete for the same HNI money. One contrarian thought: the sell-off in listed gold peers (Muthoot Finance at a 52-week low) says nothing about MFL's own book. It does make the IPO window harder to time, because the comparable valuations moved this week, not MFL's numbers.
